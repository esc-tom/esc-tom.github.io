📚 ESC-ToM Annotation Instructions

Overview & Goal

Welcome! In this study, you will review AI-generated emotional support dialogues, revise the pre-filled BDI (Belief, Desire, Intention), complete a two-step cognitive appraisal selection based on the dialogue content, and rate the dialogue quality. To get started, let's first understand the key concepts.

Key Concepts:

  • Therapeutic Dialogues: Dialogue between a therapist and a patient centered around a bothering event that the patient recently experienced.
  • Belief, Desire, Intention (BDI): The patient's mental state before the bothering event occurred.
    • Belief: What the patient thought was true before the bothering event occurred.
    • Desire: What the patient wanted to achieve before the bothering event occurred.
    • Intention: What the patient planned to do before the bothering event occurred.
  • Cognitive Appraisals: Aspects of how the patient subjectively perceives their situation after the bothering event occurred. These appraisals should make sense given the patient's pre-event BDI - meaning the way they think about the event should logically connect to what they believed, wanted, or planned before the event happened. The appraisal selection is now two-step (hierarchical): first choose relevant coarse categories (shown as descriptions), then pick exactly 5 fine-grained dimensions (name + description) and drag to rank.
  • Minimum Context Turn: The earliest turn of the dialogue where there is enough information to accurately deduce the patient's mental state (including both BDI and cognitive appraisals).

▼ Workflow Summary

Core Tasks (Required): For every dialogue, please complete the following tasks. The main focus is on revising BDI and cognitive appraisals.

1 Mark Minimum Context Turn

Goal: Select the earliest turn pair that reveals the patient's mental state. Identify where there is enough information to accurately deduce both BDI and cognitive appraisals.

  1. Read from the start.
  2. Ask: "When do I first understand the pre-event mental state?"
  3. Click that turn pair to mark it.

CRITICAL: Select the minimum context. If the patient's pre-event BDI and post-event appraisals can be deduced using dialogues up to turn 4, mark it as the minimum context turn.

Note: Up to 12 turns of dialogue are displayed, which is typically sufficient for understanding the bothering event and revising the pre-event BDI and post-event appraisals.

How to Edit: Click on the turn pair in the dialogue to select it as the minimum context.
2 Revise BDI (CORE TASK)

Goal: Revise the patient's pre-event BDI. Ensure the annotations reflect the patient's belief, desire, and intention right before the event. Do not confuse it with the patient's BDI after the event has taken place.

Logic: Negative emotions are often triggered when an event conflicts with a person's Beliefs, Desires, and Intentions (BDI).

Instruction: Revise the patient's BDI to ensure it logically explains their interpretation of the event. The BDI you write should help explain why the patient reacted the way they did - meaning their pre-event thoughts should connect to how they appraised the event afterward.

Component ❌ Needs Revision ✅ Better Version
Belief
(Specific truth) 		"I believe that I am an important person." "I believe that my family members care about my wellbeing."
Desire
(Pre-event want) 		"I wish that I can pass the exam." "I wish that I can get a good score on the exam and get into a top university."
Intention
(Actionable plan)		 "I intend to fix it." "I intend to read through the user manual and figure out how to repair the broken espresso machine."
How to Edit: Simply revise the content of the BDI fields on the right panel, the prefix is already provided for you.
3 Revise Appraisals (CORE TASK)

Goal: Complete the two-step (hierarchical) cognitive appraisal selection. First pick relevant coarse categories, then select exactly 5 fine-grained dimensions and drag to rank them by importance (1 = most important). These dimensions describe how the patient evaluates the situation after the bothering event occurred.

⚠️ Important: The cognitive appraisals you select should make sense given the patient's pre-event BDI. Think about it this way: if the patient believed something, wanted something, or planned something before the event, their reaction to the event (the appraisals) should logically connect to those pre-event thoughts. For example, if someone believed their family cared about them, and then their family ignored them, they might appraise it as "unfair" or "unexpected" - these appraisals make sense given their pre-event belief.

  • ADD if dialogue clearly supports it (e.g., "I have no idea what happens next" → Unpredictability).
  • REMOVE if dialogue contradicts it (e.g., "It's out of my hands" → Remove Self Control).
  • CHECK that your selected appraisals logically connect to the pre-event BDI you revised in Task 2.

How it works in the interface:

  • Step 1 (Coarse): Click the category items (you will see descriptions only).
  • Step 2 (Fine): From the grid (each item shows dimension name + description), choose exactly 5.
  • Rank: Drag the selected 5 in the right panel to reorder them by importance.
How to Do It: Select coarse categories first, then pick exactly 5 fine-grained dimensions and drag to rank them.
Optional Edit Utterances (Only if extremely wrong)

Note: Dialogue editing is optional and should only be done when the content is extremely wrong. Your main focus should be on revising BDI and cognitive appraisals.

When to Edit: Only fix content that is:

  • Physically impossible scenarios (e.g., seeing something that cannot be seen from the current location)
  • Direct contradictions that make the dialogue nonsensical
  • Garbled text that destroys meaning (not minor typos)

Do NOT edit: Natural slang, pauses, minor stylistic preferences, or grammatically correct sentences.

Example Utterance: "I saw my son getting bullied at school while I was in a meeting with my boss."
Issue: Physically impossible to see the school while in an office meeting.
Fix: "I was informed that my son was bullied at school while I was in a meeting with my boss."
How to Edit: Hover over the utterance and click "Edit". Modify the text and click "Save". (The text will show a yellow highlight and an (edited) marker).
4 Rate Dialogue Quality

After completing your annotations, rate the dialogue quality using the 1-5 star rating section at the bottom of the page. Rate the dialogue on realism, persona consistency, BDI association, and appraisal association.

How to Rate: Click the stars in each of the four rating criteria at the bottom of the page (1 star = poor, 5 stars = excellent).

▼ Quality Checklist

Before clicking "Save Annotation," ask yourself:

  • Is the BDI Pre-Event?
    Do the Beliefs and Desires describe how they felt before the issue started? (CORE TASK)
  • Are Appraisals Supported?
    Do the 5 selected dimensions match the patient's specific words? Do they make sense given the pre-event BDI? (CORE TASK)
  • Is the Context Precise?
    Is the green checkmark on the earliest possible turn?
  • Is the Text Clean?
    Did I fix any extremely wrong content (only if necessary)?
  • Have I Rated the Dialogue?
    Did I provide star ratings for all 4 quality criteria at the bottom of the page?

▼ FAQs

Do I have to change everything?

No. If the pre-filled BDI is already accurate, you can leave it as is and focus on carefully choosing the top 5 appraisals that best match the dialogue.

How long should this take?

Roughly 3-5 minutes per dialogue. You don't need to edit the dialogue content unless it is extremely wrong.

What if I accidentally closed the annotation website?

Don't worry. Return to the prolific study page and click the same link to resume your annotation.
